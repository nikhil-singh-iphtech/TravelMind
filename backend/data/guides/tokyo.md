Tokyo blends centuries-old tradition with relentless modernity. Asakusa's Senso-ji temple district offers a glimpse of old Edo, with narrow lantern-lit streets selling traditional crafts and street snacks. For a quieter cultural morning, Meiji Shrine sits inside a forested park in the middle of Shibuya, a striking contrast to the neon crossing just outside.

Outdoor options cluster around the city's rivers and parks. A walk along the Sumida River at dusk takes in several of the bridges locals use for evening runs, and Ueno Park's wide paths are popular for cycling and picnics, especially during cherry blossom season in late March and early April.

Food culture here rewards wandering: izakaya alleys in Shinjuku's Omoide Yokocho serve grilled skewers in tiny stalls seating only a handful of people, while the outer market area near the old Tsukiji site still runs a lively morning trade even though the main wholesale market relocated to Toyosu.

Public transport is extensive and punctual. Most neighborhoods worth visiting sit within a short walk of a subway or train station, which makes staying almost anywhere central a reasonable choice.
